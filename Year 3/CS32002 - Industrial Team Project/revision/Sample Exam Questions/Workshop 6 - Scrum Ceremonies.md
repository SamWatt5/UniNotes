### Scenario
A software company is developing a new incident response platform for managing outages in cloud infrastructure. The product is being developed using Scrum with two-week sprints. The team consists of one Product Owner, one Scrum Master, and six Developers.

The team is currently halfway through Sprint 4.

The Sprint Goal is:

**“Provide a working incident timeline view so that engineers can understand what happened during a service outage.”**

The Sprint Backlog includes the following items:

- Incident timeline visualisation
- Event log ingestion service
- Alert correlation logic
- Basic filtering controls
- Initial usability improvements

During the sprint, several issues begin to appear:

- Two developers realise that they have been implementing different interpretations of how the event correlation logic should work.
- A developer mentions during a casual conversation that the event ingestion service may not scale to production volumes, but this has not been raised formally with the team.
- Senior management ask the Product Owner for an early demonstration of the timeline interface before the sprint ends, as they want to show progress to external stakeholders.

During internal discussions, the team says they are “mostly on track,” but no one has yet attempted to run the full timeline system using realistic event data.

The Sprint Review is scheduled in four days.
### Question
You are asked to advise the team. Write a structured response addressing the following:
(a) Explain how the Daily Scrum should be used in this situation to surface the problems emerging within the team. (10 marks)

(b) Explain how the Sprint Review should be conducted given the current uncertainty around system behaviour and management expectations. (10 marks)

(c) Explain how the Sprint Retrospective could help the team improve their delivery process after this sprint. (5 marks)
