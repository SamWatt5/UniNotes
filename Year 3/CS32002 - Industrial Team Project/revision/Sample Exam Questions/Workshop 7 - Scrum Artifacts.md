### Scenario
A technology company is developing a new platform for coordinating emergency response during large-scale infrastructure failures. The system will integrate data from sensors, communication networks, and response teams to provide a real-time operational view during incidents. The product is being developed using Scrum with two-week sprints.

The team consists of:

- one Product Owner
- one Scrum Master
- five Developers

The team has recently begun Sprint 3. The Sprint Goal is:

“Provide an initial operational dashboard that allows emergency coordinators to view incoming alerts and track response activity.”

The Sprint Backlog includes the following items:

- Alert ingestion service
- Incident dashboard interface
- Response team status tracker
- Alert filtering and prioritisation controls
- Integration with the existing monitoring API

During the sprint several issues begin to appear.

Two developers are working on different components of the alert ingestion service, but each has assumed a different format for incoming event data. This inconsistency is discovered only when attempting to connect the components together.

The Scrum Master asks the team for an update on sprint progress. Individual developers report that their tasks are “almost finished,” but no one has attempted to run the full system end-to-end.

During a conversation with the Product Owner, a developer asks what criteria must be satisfied before the dashboard can be considered complete. The team realises that they have not agreed on a clear Definition of Done for the sprint.

At the same time, the Product Owner notices that some work being discussed by developers does not appear in the Sprint Backlog.

The Sprint Review is scheduled in three days.

### Question
(a) Explain the role of the Product Backlog in maintaining shared understanding of the work that may be required for the product. (8 marks)
(b) Explain the role of the Sprint Backlog in providing visibility of the team’s current work. (8 marks)
(c) The team realises they have not defined a clear Definition of Done. Explain:
- what the Definition of Done is
- why it is important for Scrum teams
- how it would help prevent the team from incorrectly assuming that work is complete in this scenario

(9 marks)
