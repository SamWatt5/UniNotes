### Question Scenario
A regional NHS hospital is undertaking a major upgrade of its ageing Electronic Patient Record (EPR) system. The current system remains operational but has become unstable, with increasing reports of slow performance and intermittent errors. The hospital board has approved a 12-week implementation window and a fixed budget. Development capacity is limited and cannot be expanded.

The upgrade backlog includes the following competing priorities:

- Security patching of legacy components
- Migration of 15 years of historical patient data
- Integration with the pharmacy prescription system
- User interface improvements requested by clinicians
- Compliance reporting enhancements
- Executive performance dashboards
- Full disaster recovery testing
- AI-driven bed occupancy forecasting

Recent developments have increased pressure on the programme:

- A minor but contained data breach was reported to regulators.
- Two near-miss prescription errors were linked to confusing interface layouts.
- Local media have criticised long patient waiting times.
- Senior executives have requested improved performance reporting visibility.

The Chief Digital Officer has made clear that not all backlog items can be completed within the 12-week window. A prioritised delivery plan must now be agreed.
### Question
You are acting as the Product Owner for the EPR upgrade.

Write a structured response addressing the following:

1. Identify and justify the three most critical priorities that must be delivered within the 12-week window. (10 marks)
2. Explain which significant items you would defer, and justify the risks that are being deliberately accepted by doing so. (8 marks)
3. Describe and justify a structured prioritisation approach you would use to support and defend your decisions to stakeholders. Your answer should make explicit reference to trade-offs, risk exposure, and value under constraint. (7 marks)
