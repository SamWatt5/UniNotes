## What is Agile?  
- **Agile = philosophy + mindset** (not a single methodology)  
- Emphasises:  
- Collaboration between business & developers  
- Iterative + incremental delivery  
- Continuous feedback  
- Adapting to changing requirements  
- Opposes heavy sequential waterfall planning  
### Agile characteristics  
- Timeboxed delivery  
- Cross-functional teams  
- Self-organised teams  
- Working software focus  
- Lightweight process  
### Common Agile frameworks  
- Scrum  
- Extreme Programming (XP)  
- Kanban  
- Lean  
- Crystal  
- DSDM  
- FDD  
## History of Agile  
- Roots before 2001:  
- Toyota Production System  
- Adaptive Software Development (1974)  
- Theory of Constraints (1992)  
- Scrum (Sutherland & Schwaber)  
- XP (Kent Beck)  
### Agile Manifesto — 2001  
- 17 practitioners met at Snowbird, Utah  
- Formed Agile Alliance  
- Goal: alternative to heavy documentation-driven development  
## The Agile Manifesto (Core Values)  
### 1. Individuals & Interactions > Processes & Tools  
- Communication beats rigid process  
- Face-to-face preferred  
- Processes still exist — just secondary  
### 2. Working Software > Comprehensive Documentation  
- Only working code provides value  
- Documentation should be barely sufficient  
- Documents ≠ progress  
### 3. Customer Collaboration > Contract Negotiation  
- Flexible partnership  
- Adapt requirements continuously  
- Trust over legal rigidity  
### 4. Responding to Change > Following a Plan  
- Change is expected  
- Use rolling wave planning  
- Re-plan frequently instead of locking upfront  
## Key Agile Concepts  
### Iterative Development  
- Deliver small increments regularly  
- Gather feedback early  
### Incremental Delivery  
- Each iteration adds usable value  
### Rolling Wave Planning  
- Plan near future in detail  
- Plan distant future loosely  
### Fail-Fast  
- Prototype → feedback → refine quickly  
# The 12 Agile Principles  
## 1 — Customer Satisfaction  
- Early + continuous delivery of valuable software  
## 2 — Welcome Change  
- Even late changes improve competitiveness  
## 3 — Frequent Delivery  
- Every few weeks preferred  
## 4 — Business + Developers Work Daily  
- Continuous collaboration  
## 5 — Motivated Individuals  
- Trust teams to self-organise  
## 6 — Face-to-Face Communication  
- Most effective knowledge transfer  
## 7 — Working Software = Progress Metric  
- Only real measurement of progress  
## 8 — Sustainable Pace  
- Avoid burnout  
- Maintain constant velocity  
## 9 — Technical Excellence  
- Good design increases agility  
- Manage technical debt  
## 10 — Simplicity  
- Maximise work NOT done  
- Focus on value  
## 11 — Self-Organising Teams  
- Architecture emerges from teams  
## 12 — Regular Reflection (Retrospectives)  
- Inspect & adapt continuously  
# Declaration of Interdependence (DOI)  
Focus: Agile leadership & management  
Key ideas:  
- Continuous value delivery  
- Customer engagement  
- Manage uncertainty via iterations  
- Empower people  
- Shared accountability  
- Situational processes  
# Waterfall vs Agile  
## Waterfall  
Characteristics  
- Sequential phases  
- Fixed scope  
- Heavy documentation  
- Late testing  
- Expensive change  
Best for  
- Stable, clear requirements  
## Agile  
Characteristics  
- Iterative cycles  
- Flexible scope  
- Continuous testing  
- Customer feedback  
- Adaptive planning  
Best for  
- Uncertain / evolving requirements  
# Stacey Matrix (When to use Agile)  
Simple (clear + agreed) → Waterfall  
Complex / uncertain → Agile  
Chaotic → Neither  
# Benefits of Agile  
- Faster value delivery  
- Reduced change cost  
- Increased quality  
- Better visibility  
- Higher team motivation  
- Lower risk  
# Limitations of Agile  
- Hard to estimate upfront cost  
- Requires experienced teams  
- Needs customer availability  
- Less documentation risk  
- Tooling overhead
