# What is Agile?
- agile is:
	- a philosophy
	- a mindset
	- not a methodology
	- embodies practices, tools and a culture that allows the business/technology team to closely collaborate and thrive in a zone of rapidly changing requirements, and to deliver working code in an incremental and iterative manner.
	- an alternative to traditional project delivery that mostly uses a sequential or waterfall model
	- uses a timeboxed approach to frequently deliver product increments and seek continuous feedback from the users - thereby being able to refine the system.
	- inherently lightweight
	- encourages teamwork between a set of cross-functional, self-organised and empowered members to deliver high-quality software.
- Popular methodologies that follow the Agile values and principles:
	- Extreme Programming (XP)
	- Scrum
	- Kanban
	- Lean
	- Crystal
	- Dynamic Systems Development Method (DSDM)
	- Feature Driven Development (FDD)
- These values and principles are stated in the Agile Manifesto.
# History of Agile
- The 1990s saw rapid proliferation of demand or software systems to manage evolving business needs
	- Teams took the comfort of heavy waterfall based processes to deliver software
	- it was quickly realised that the lag between conceiving an idea to the delivery of the product must be reduced to withstand fierce competition in the market
- Most practitioners think the Agile Manifesto (2001) is the most important and initial milestone of the Agile journey.
	- that is not the case.
	- the manifesto was written by experts who were advocating various lightweight methodologies for delivering software.
	- some existed many years before the manifesto was created.
- In 1974, E.A Edmonds wrote a paper on the adaptive software development process
- Some of the roots can be traced back to Toyota's production system.
- The 1990's saw more action.
	- The Theory of Constraints was published in 1992, speaking about identifying bottlenecks in a system and targeting all efforts to remove the same
	- In the mid-90s, Scrum was introduced to the world, delivering software through short timeboxed iterations that are preceeded by a planning game and ending with a demo and retrospective. Scrum is arguable the most prevalent methodology in the Agile community
	- Extreme Programming (XP) was introduced around the same time,  being a lightweight methodology for small-to-medium sized teams developing software in the face of vague or rapidy changing requirements
	- Other methodologies also sprung up, namely Unified Framework, Dynamic Systems Development Method, Feature-Driven Development and Alistair Cockburn's Crystal family of methodologies
- What was common between flavours was the lightweight, but sufficient practices and emphasis on close collaboration and communication between the delivery teams and business users.
# The Agile Manifesto
## The Four Core Values of the Agile Manifesto
1. **Individuals and interaction** over processes and tools
2. **Working software** over comprehensive documentation
3. **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation
4. **Responding to change** over following a plan
### Individuals and Interactions over Processes and Tools
- This emphasizes the people in the team and interactions within rather than a heavy-process mindset and an armory of project management tools
- This comes from the fact that in contrast to operations or manufacturing industries, software engineering being an inherent knowledge-based work cannot afford to undermine the power of collaboration and individuals talking to each other.
- The value doesn't say that processes are an overhead or cannot help in delivering projects, nor does it say that Agile projects (of any scale) are completely devoid of processes and tools.
- But, be it trawling requirements from customers, elucidating scope to analysts and developers, triaging and troubleshooting defects it is the people who plays the dominant role.
- The focus should be on people and not on just fulfilling the "heavy-weight" process requirement that might not be even applicable to all real-world situations.
