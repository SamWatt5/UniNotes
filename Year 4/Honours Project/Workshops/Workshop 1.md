# What is the Honours Project?
An extended, independent project in which you **specify, design, implement and evaluate** a substantial computing artefact that addresses a clearly defined problem or research question.
1. Critically evaluate literature, technical documentation, standards and existing systems
2. Define a complex computing problem or research question at honours level
3. Design, implement and test a substantial artefact using appropriate methods and professional practice
4. Critically evaluate the effectiveness and limitations of your solution using evidence
5. Communicate technical work effectively in writing and in person
## Two project types
>[!tip] Software development
>Focus on engineering practice and implementation quality to solve a real-world problem or meet a documented need.
>**For example:**
>- Website or application with a clear feature set
>- Accessibility-focused implementation
>- AI-enabled application
>- API development

>[!abstract] Research
>Focus on research methods - answering a research question through the creation of an artefact
>**For example:**
>- Prototype to test a hypothesis
>- Novel interaction technique
>- ML experiment pipeline
>- Dataset generation
## What you'll submit
1. **Interim report** - Formative - no grade
	- *Week 13*
	- Literature review and project plan
	- Marked by your supervisor using a rubric
	- Written feedback to feed into your final report
2. **Final portfolio** - 100% of module grade
	- *Week 24, Friday 12 noon*
	- Written report of 15-35 pages, including references
	- Your artefact - typically a codebase
	- Relevant appendices, e.g. ethics and study materials, visuals, engineering documentation
3. **Demonstration** - Pass/Fail - nyst be passed
	- *Week 25 (exam week 1), TBC*
	- Up to 5 min demo + up to 10 min Q&A
	- Confirms understanding and authorship of your work
	- Both markers must agree you've passed
## Where the marks go
**Software development**
- 20% - Problem analysis, requirements & context
- 20% - System design & technical approach
- 30% - Artefact implementation & technical quality
- 20% - Testing & evaluation
- 10% - Dissertation quality
**Research**
- 25% - Resewarch quesiton, rationale & literature review
- 25% - Research design & methodological rigour
- 20% - Artefact design & implementation
- 20% - Analysis, evaluation & conclusion
- 10% - Dissertation quality
*The dissertation writing iteself is only 10%*: most marks come from the thinking, justification and evaluation you show.
## LSEP runs through everything
**Legal**
- Data protection (UK GDPR), licensing and IP, accessibility law
**Social**
- Who benefits, who's excluded? Inclusion, accessibility, wider impact
**Ethical**
- Informed consent, participant wellbeing, bias in data and AI
**Professional**
- BCS Code of Conduct, security, testing, honest reporting of results

*Think about these from week 1. They should shape your requirements, design and evaluation.*
## How your project is marked
**Final Portfolio**
1. Supervisor
	- Marks your portfolio
2. Second marker
	- Not involved in supervision, marks it independently
3. Average
	- of the two marks is your grade
4. Moderator
	- Appointed if marks differ by 3+ grade points or across pass/fail

**Demonstration**
1. Demo + Q&A
	- Up to 5 min demo, up to 10 min questions
2. Both markers agree
	- You understand and own the work
3. Second opportunity
	- If concerns remain, there will be a further, more in-depth session
4. Misconduct review
	- Only if authorship concerns remain after that
*If a moderator asks for an extra demonstration, it can't lower your mark below the lower of the two markers' grades*
## What we expect of you
**You drive supervision**
- Minimum 8 hours of meetings, at least 4 of them one-to-one. You request the meetings and set the agenda.
**Come to the workshops**
- Six 2-hour workshops cover the skills behind each part of the rubric.
**Work professionally**
- Use version control from day one, keep a project log, and back everything up.
**Appropriate AI use (Amber)**
- Permitted for brainstorming, drafting and structuring - but acknowledge it, and review and critically evaluate anything you use.
**Know your stuff**
- In the demo you'll be asked to explain your code, design and results, If you can't explain it, don't submit it.
**Set your own deadlines**
- The final deadline is far away. You need to break the wokr into smaller tasks that are manageable.

# The project canvas
## why start with a canvas?
| On your canvas                               | Interim report criterion               |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Aim, objectives, outcomes, success measures  | Problem definition / research question |
| What I know, don't know, where I'll find out | Context and literature                 |
| Approach & methods, tools & resources        | Methodology / technical approach       |
| Risks, first actions                         | Project plan and feasability           |
| Ethics & LSEP                                | Professional considerations (LSEP)     |
## The project canvas
1. Direction
	- Aim
		- The one overarching thing your project will achieve or find out.
	- Objectives
		- 3-5 specific, measurable steps that together deliver the aim.
	- Outcomes & deliverables
		- What will exist at the end? Artefact, findings, data, dissertation.
	- Measures of success
		- How will you know? Tests, metrics, user feedback, an answer to your question.
2. Knowledge
	- What I already know
		- Skills, technologies, topics and prior work you're confident with.
	- What I don't know yet
		- Gaps in knowledge, skills or technology you'll need to fill.
	- Where I'll find out
		- Literature, documentation, standards, datasets, existing systems.
	- Who can help
		- Supervisor, users, clients, domain experts, participants.
3. Delivery
	- Approach & methods
		- Development methodology or research design - and why it fits.
	- Tools & resources
		- Languages, frameworks, hardware, data, compute, access you need.
	- Ethics & LSEP
		- People or personal data involved? Legal, social, ethical, professional issues.
	- Risks & mitigations
		- What could stop you? How likely, how serious - and what's plan B?
## Direction: where are you heading?
**Aim**
- One sentence: the overall purpose of the project
- *starts with a verb: design, build, evaluate, investigate, compare...*
**Objectives**
- 3-5 concrete steps that together achieve the aim.
- *Each one you could tick off as done. Think SMART*
**Outcomes**
- The tangible things that exist at the end.
- *Artefact, test suite, dataset, findings, dissertation.*
**Success measures**
- The evidence that tells you each objective was met.
- *Requirements met? Tests pass? Users succeed? Question answered?*

*Software development*: success is usually requirements met and evaluated with users.
*Research*: success is a rigorous answer to your question.
## Worked example: software development
**Problem**: Volunteers at a community sports club take court bookings by phone and a sared spreadsheet - double bookings are common and the online form isn't accessible to members who use screen readers.

**Aim**: Design build and evaluate an accessible web-based booking system that meets the club's needs and WCAG 2.2 AA.

**Objectives**
1. Review existing booking systems and accessibility guidance
2. Elicit and prioritise requirements with the club (MoSCoW)
3. Implement booking, cancellation and admin features iteratively
4. Test functionality and accessibility
5. Evaluate usability with club members

**Outcomes**
- Deployed web app and repository
- Automated test suite
- Accessibility audit report
- Usability findings and dissertation

**Success measures**
- All 'Must' requirements implemented and passing tests
- No WCAG 2.2 AA failures in audit
- Members complete a booking unaided in usability testing
- Committee sign-off
